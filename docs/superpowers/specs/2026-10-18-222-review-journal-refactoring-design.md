## 1. Purpose and owner direction

AIPR is a moderator for agent review. It coordinates review turns, records the results, and formats review documentation consistently so humans and agents can audit it. Review agents inspect the submitted artifact and relevant codebase. Their output is review documentation; they do not implement changes to the codebase under review.

The owner explicitly rejected execution proof as a product purpose and requested an audit and refactoring of already committed support, followed by reconciliation of open stories before their execution. This specification implements that direction. It replaces the proposed direction of #216 if adopted; the earlier document, review, adoption statement, and completed-story history remain historical records.

Success means that a useful review can be moderated and audited without producer-image attestation, proof publication, or approval of an execution-proof chain. Current records identify their format and validate against its documented schema. Historical records remain readable as historical documents with their associated schemas, without a promise that every historical workflow remains executable.

## 2. Scope and alternatives

Selected approach: remove the unnecessary proof/approval layer at its actual consumer boundaries, reuse existing journal/schema facilities, and preserve coordination mechanisms whose behavior has a concrete operational purpose.

Rejected alternatives:

1. Rename proofs to journals while retaining the same execution-attestation gates. This preserves the unwanted mechanism.
2. Delete everything named evidence, authority, lineage, or receipt. This would remove useful audit history and may break turn routing, safe recovery, or unrelated development governance.

In scope:

- Inventory committed code, tests, schemas, generated data, CI/release commands, documentation, public exports, and downstream consumers associated with review proof or approval authority.
- Determine each item's actual consumer and shipped status before changing it.
- Remove unnecessary proof generation, execution-attestation admission, and publication prerequisites.
- Preserve or simplify the current review journal and current-format validation.
- Enforce the review-only role boundary through existing supported mechanisms and honest instructions.
- Reconcile affected open issues, accepted design/plan pointers, ACs, verification commands, dependencies, and aggregate completion obligations.

Out of scope:

- A new generic evidence engine, cryptographic attestation service, schema framework, policy engine, database redesign, or migration program.
- Implementation or delivery by participants in an AIPR review session.
- Unrelated knowledge retrieval, analytics, experimentation, or global-install redesign.
- Activation of #107, Windows support, or operating on protected unsettled sessions.

## 3. Product boundary

### 3.1 Review and write behavior

- AIPR routes the selected artifact and relevant context to review participants and collects their responses, findings, dispositions, and recorded decisions.
- Review participants may write only their designated current review-documentation outputs. They may not edit application source, dependencies, configuration, Git metadata, another participant's response, or earlier sealed records.
- AIPR-owned writes are limited to its declared coordination state and designated review documentation. AIPR does not implement source changes or commit the reviewed codebase.
- If an external author revises the artifact, AIPR records the new artifact identity/version and a subsequent review. That revision belongs to the external authoring workflow, not an AIPR permission to modify the subject. The registered submitting participant supplies the revised artifact reference and expected prior review revision/subject digest through an explicit handoff. AIPR reads and snapshots those bytes; it does not apply a patch to the subject. Existing role/session authentication remains required.
- An artifact change during a reviewer turn must refuse stale advancement and cannot silently replace the reviewed subject. Preserve recorded responses and snapshots as history about their original digests; a later edit does not retroactively erase or relabel that history. While drift is unresolved, preserve the unfinished turn as incomplete. If the external author restores the exact authorized bytes, the existing valid submission/retry path remains available; AIPR does not restore the subject itself. Any supersession requires an adopted operation under the applicable versioned contract. A revision handoff must bind the new digest, preserve prior responses, reject stale submissions and retain duplicate-safe retries. An unchanged-artifact response remains possible with an explicit disposition; neither a revision nor a new acceptance is inferred from silence. The exact supported existing operation or necessary versioned change must be adopted before its implementation.
- Review-only participant behavior is a separate workstream from process observation and owner recovery. Current author artifact-write instructions, CLI submission behavior, launch restrictions, and #118/#148–#150 author-capable requirements must be reconciled together; removing a write permission alone is insufficient.
- Existing flows that let an AIPR author participant edit the subject must be assessed explicitly against this boundary. Removing proof alone does not resolve that conflict.
- Supported provider path restrictions may enforce the boundary. Existing manual sessions remain instruction-bound where the host offers no enforcement. Do not build a new sandbox or claim that instructions prove write denial.
- A controller cannot manufacture a participant's findings or acceptance. Exit, silence, timeout, successful JSON validation, and schema compatibility are not review acceptance.

### 3.2 What records establish

A record describes the activity reported and recorded by AIPR: the artifact version, participants, turns, findings, responses, decisions, timestamps when available, and recorded outcome. Validation establishes supported format and internal consistency. Artifact hashes associate records with bytes and can detect changed bytes when compared with retained values.

These checks do not independently establish provider execution, model execution, uncompromised host behavior, or a cryptographic reviewer identity. Requested, declared, observed, unavailable, and uncertain values retain their original meanings. Do not strengthen them by changing labels.

### 3.3 Coordination mechanisms to preserve

Keep mechanisms needed for correct moderation:

- Review/project/role/session/turn association and the existing local authentication needed to prevent misrouting.
- One writer for a state transition, expected-revision checks, atomic persistence, and duplicate-safe retry behavior.
- Artifact/response version association, prior-response preservation, and explicit lifecycle outcomes.
- Read-only diagnosis and supported recovery of a pending turn without launching duplicate participants.
- Ownership checks that prevent taking a live lock or terminating an unrelated process. Timeout or endpoint silence alone does not establish process death.
- Ordinary supported filesystem access and containment checks for AIPR's own writes.

Keep a mechanism because a named behavior requires it, not because an old proof contract requires a field. Development issue approvals and CI integrity remain separate from the product journal; this refactoring does not bypass the repository's governed delivery workflow.

## 4. Versioned record contract

### 4.1 Use existing formats first

Existing `schemas/event-v1.json`, `schemas/event-v2.json`, `schemas/manifest-v1.json`, participant/response schemas, renderers, and validators are the starting point. Do not introduce a new format merely to rename the system.

Inventory each currently emitted durable document. Its machine-readable data must identify its schema family and version, interpreted in its documented operation/document-family context. Human-readable review documents must carry a documented schema/format identifier in their metadata. The associated schema document must explain structure and field meanings. A package version or producer image digest is not a schema version.

Add missing metadata only in the appropriate versioned format. A breaking field or semantic change requires a new schema version; do not silently change the meaning of an already published version. Historical schema definitions and their explanatory documents remain available. The same versioning rule applies to process-source class/ledger, conformance/registration and persisted ownership/creation-source records, not only review documents. Removing required review-approval fields from a class requires a new class version; an affected ledger or persisted seal contract likewise requires an explicit versioned change. Preserve the original v1 schemas, ledger bytes and recorded seals as history rather than rewriting them under their old identifiers. Fixing documentation errors may be additive with an explicit erratum; do not rewrite historical bytes to make an old record validate differently.

### 4.2 Current reader and writer behavior

- The current writer emits the current documented version for each document family.
- The current validator dispatches through a fixed package-owned set of supported operation/document-family and schema-identifier combinations. The API envelope uses schema artifact `ai-peer-review.api-response/v1` while its transport tag is `ai-peer-review.response/v1`; the current participant-response writer also uses that transport identifier with `schemas/response-v1.json`. This participant format remains current and also interprets historical responses; it is not retired by this cleanup. Preserve the adopted contextual dispatch rule and both schema artifacts. A tag alone must not select a reader.
- Use existing event validation, event reduction and document-specific checks for journal interpretation. `src/api/validate.mjs:validateContract` is an API-contract subset, not a JSON Schema evaluator for journal documents: it does not resolve `$ref` or evaluate `anyOf`, and its `oneOf` branch returns before sibling constraints. Do not route journal schemas through it or advertise full JSON Schema validation without a separately justified, bounded implementation. Existing API validation is not weakened by this cleanup.
- A fixed package-owned registry may select only supported local document checks. An input cannot select executable code, a retained runtime, or a remote schema download.
- Validate required fields, supported values, associated review/artifact/turn references, and applicable recorded transition consistency.
- Distinguish malformed input, unsupported format, incomplete records, and well-formed records with a non-accepted outcome using each existing format's real enums. A reviewer rejection or revision request is valid response/event data; it need not be a terminal journal status. Existing `buildManifest`/manifest-v1 accepts terminal acceptance variants only. Pending, abandoned, superseded or otherwise non-accepted event histories are inspected at their journal/response boundary, not forced into acceptance manifests. Introducing another terminal outcome or changing manifest semantics requires an explicit versioned decision. A recorded non-acceptance is not itself malformed.
- Validation must not require provider execution, model attestation, an exact installed producer image, a source-class approval, a Git finalization commit as evidence of reviewer acceptance, a primary enrollment operation, or a published proof bundle.
- Unknown/future schemas produce an explicit unsupported-format result without modifying the input. Missing schema metadata is reported as such; no guessed version or automatic migration.
- Existing historical readers may remain where useful and inexpensive. New development is not obligated to add historical runtime replay, retained-image execution, automatic migration, or universal old-version acceptance.
- Validation of a recorded outcome does not approve an artifact or satisfy a consuming system's approval policy. External consumers make their own explicit workflow decisions from the recorded data.

### 4.3 Repository context and acceptance provenance

Repository commit provenance is useful review context, separate from execution attestation or source-class approval. Record full commit object IDs, never abbreviated SHAs or moving branch names as substitutes. A commit identifies a repository snapshot; the FUR digest identifies the exact reviewed bytes, including frontmatter. Neither value substitutes for the other. A dirty worktree can contain several FUR revisions at the same commit.

- **Review-response Markdown:** Every newly written reviewer response includes `reviewer_commit` in its YAML frontmatter: the HEAD of the reviewer's subject-code checkout when the reviewer establishes the context used for that response. Bind it to that response's review, turn and FUR digest. Existing `artifact_commit` describes the submitted artifact's context and must not be silently reinterpreted as the reviewer's checkout. If the reviewer changes checkout commits during a turn, reconcile and record the final context actually evaluated before submission; do not leave a stale start value or substitute the controller's HEAD. An unavailable commit remains explicitly unavailable and the response incomplete for this provenance requirement; never fabricate a SHA.
- **FUR Markdown:** Every externally authored revision of the File Under Review includes a unique, stable `revision_id` within its artifact lineage and `based_on_commit` in its YAML frontmatter: the subject repository HEAD used as the basis when that revision was written. Set it before snapshotting or reviewing the revision, and preserve it with the exact revision bytes. An unchanged FUR retains its original basis even if the branch later advances. The external author updates this metadata when writing a revision; AIPR does not edit the FUR to add it. The writing-basis SHA does not mean the FUR already exists in that commit. The external publication workflow commits each completed review turn as specified in §4.4; AIPR itself does not commit the subject. For non-Markdown subjects, adopt an explicit equivalent versioned metadata representation before supporting this requirement; do not inject YAML into source files.
- **Acceptance manifest:** Each final acceptance of a FUR under SAR, SPR or XPR records `acceptance_head`, the subject branch HEAD at the time that acceptance is recorded. Bind every entry to its review ID, review pattern, accepted FUR digest/revision, recorded decision/basis and acceptance time. Retain separate entries for successive review patterns and review instances, even when their SHAs coincide. Preserve existing entries rather than overwriting them with the latest acceptance. Preserve the repository and branch context needed to interpret the SHA; distinguish a detached HEAD explicitly. A recorded review-pattern label does not strengthen participant independence or identity claims.
- Capture acceptance HEAD from the subject worktree at the acceptance transition, with a consistency check that refuses/retries if the relevant HEAD or accepted FUR changes during capture. Do not record a later documentation/publication commit as the earlier acceptance HEAD. A missing or unavailable acceptance SHA remains explicit and cannot satisfy the complete provenance requirement. Preserve any already recorded participant decision without presenting incomplete provenance as complete acceptance documentation. An acceptance SHA is an observation of repository context, not a Git finalization requirement or proof that the worktree was clean.
- **Later plan generation:** Before deriving a plan, select the applicable accepted review layers under the owner's approval policy, verify the current FUR digest against those accepted bytes, resolve their recorded acceptance SHAs in the correct repository, and inspect the diff from each applicable `acceptance_head` to the current subject branch HEAD. Inspect relevant uncommitted changes as well; a commit-to-commit diff cannot reveal them. Record the selected acceptances, compared SHAs and effect of intervening changes on specification assumptions. A nonempty diff does not automatically invalidate acceptance; material changes require revised specification review or an explicit owner disposition before relying on that acceptance. Missing objects, missing provenance or changed FUR bytes cannot silently pass this check. A changed FUR cannot inherit acceptance of earlier bytes.

Use the existing response, manifest, lineage and snapshot facilities where their documented semantics fit. Adopt explicit versioned field contracts and reader/writer changes wherever these requirements add mandatory metadata or change manifest structure; do not insert required fields into published v1 contracts silently. Retained historical responses, FUR snapshots and acceptance manifests remain unchanged. Report missing historical provenance as missing, rather than backfilling current HEAD or pretending a prior review accepted these new requirements. This specification's current frontmatter records its actual writing basis and revision ID; earlier acceptance remains scoped to the previously accepted bytes.

### 4.4 Review collateral, turn commits and reconstruction

Required review collateral comprises (1) review-response Markdown, (2) author-response Markdown, (3) the review manifest and (4) one separately named reversible FUR diff per committed revision. The revised FUR is committed with the turn's collateral. The manifest is part of the retained review package, not merely transient coordination state. Its versioned current-state representation must support in-progress review turns without misrepresenting them as terminal acceptance; retain manifest-v1 acceptance semantics and adopt the necessary explicit new format before implementation.

- **Revision chain:** Each changed FUR receives a unique `revision_id` in its frontmatter. Store one forward diff from the exact preceding FUR revision to the resulting revision, including frontmatter changes. Manifest entries record the predecessor/result revision IDs and byte digests, patch direction, and unique diff path/digest. The first revision-producing turn compares against the draft actually reviewed, whether committed, dirty or newly created; it must not substitute different committed bytes or an empty file. No separate initial-session commit, retained initial snapshot or cumulative baseline diff is required for reconstruction. The earliest draft is recoverable by reversing the first revision diff.
- **Every cycle:** Retain both participant responses, the manifest's turn record and that cycle's FUR diff, and commit them with the revised FUR. Each response identifies the FUR revision/digest it discusses; the review-response may concern the preceding revision while the author-response introduces the next one. Preserve those distinct associations. An unchanged-artifact cycle explicitly records that disposition, reuses the unchanged revision ID and records an empty change result. Missing responses remain explicitly incomplete; the controller does not fabricate participant prose to complete a bundle. The current FUR is updated in place; separate full FUR snapshots for each revision are not required as reconstruction collateral. Existing internal snapshots used for turn coordination may remain without becoming a second archival requirement.
- **Exact reversible patches:** Diffs preserve exact content, encoding and line endings; verification must reject lossy patch generation/application. Preserve file addition, deletion or path changes explicitly where supported. Unsupported subjects fail explicitly rather than emitting an unreconstructable patch. Patch reversal verifies both expected input and resulting digests instead of guessing or applying fuzzy matches. Unique revision/turn filenames preserve every diff across subsequent commits.
- **Turn publication:** An authorized external authoring/publication workflow commits each completed cycle's revised FUR and its complete collateral together in a dedicated review-turn commit, without unrelated staged changes. AIPR prepares and checks its designated documentation and reports publication state; review participants and AIPR do not acquire Git mutation permission. A failed or interrupted commit leaves publication pending with a safe, duplicate-aware retry; it does not erase a recorded participant decision or claim durable publication. A recorded decision and successful turn publication are separate facts. There is no requirement to store each turn's resulting commit SHA or maintain a turn-commit index for reconstruction. The response/FUR context SHAs and acceptance HEADs in §4.3 remain required for their separate purpose; a later publication commit never replaces the previously captured `acceptance_head`.
- **Squash-safe retention:** Keep the final accepted FUR and all separately named diffs, responses and manifest records as ordinary files in the tree merged into the parent/default branch. A squash merge can then discard intermediate Git history without discarding the revision chain. Separate initial/per-turn FUR snapshots and independently stored revision commit SHAs are unnecessary. A later review pattern continues or explicitly links the existing revision chain across SAR/SPR/XPR boundaries rather than resetting it.
- **Reconstruction:** Starting with the exact last accepted FUR, verify its recorded digest and reverse the retained diffs in reverse revision order. Check the expected digest and `revision_id` at every step to recover each preceding revision through the earliest reviewed draft. No initial baseline file, cumulative patch, original review-turn commit or per-revision Git object is needed. Preserve the final accepted bytes while reconstructing in a separate destination. Missing diffs, broken links or digest mismatches produce an explicit incomplete/corrupt history result without guessing.

FUR reconstruction and repository-context comparison are different capabilities. The retained package can reconstruct FUR revisions after squash, but FUR-only diffs cannot reconstruct the entire repository at an unreachable acceptance SHA. The later planning check in §4.3 must still report unavailable repository history explicitly; it must not claim that reconstructing the FUR proves the surrounding code is unchanged.

### 4.5 Data minimization

Retain useful audit information without adding unrestricted provider output, credentials, private session handles, or private filesystem details to public exports. Existing sensitive historical records are not republished by this cleanup. A routine copy/export, if still required by a concrete consumer, is a versioned data interface rather than an attestation.
