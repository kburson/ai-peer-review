## 1. Purpose and owner direction

AIPR is a moderator for agent review. It coordinates review turns, records the results, and formats review documentation consistently so humans and agents can audit it. Review agents inspect the submitted artifact and relevant codebase. Their output is review documentation; they do not implement changes to the codebase under review.

The owner explicitly rejected execution proof as a product purpose and requested an audit and refactoring of already committed support, followed by reconciliation of open stories before their execution. This specification implements that direction. It replaces the proposed direction of #216 if adopted; the earlier document, review, adoption statement, and completed-story history remain historical records.

Success means that a useful review can be moderated and audited without producer-image attestation, proof publication, or approval of an execution-proof chain. Current records identify their format and validate against its documented schema. Historical records remain readable as historical documents with their associated schemas, without a promise that every historical workflow remains executable.

## 2. Scope and alternatives

Selected approach: remove the unnecessary proof/approval layer at its actual consumer boundaries, reuse existing journal/schema facilities, and preserve coordination mechanisms whose behavior has a concrete operational purpose.

Rejected alternatives:

1. Rename proofs to journals while retaining the same execution-attestation gates. This preserves the unwanted mechanism.
2. Delete everything named evidence, authority, lineage, or receipt. This would remove useful audit history and may break turn routing, safe recovery, or unrelated development governance.

In scope:

- Inventory committed code, tests, schemas, generated data, CI/release commands, documentation, public exports, and downstream consumers associated with review proof or approval authority.
- Determine each item's actual consumer and shipped status before changing it.
- Remove unnecessary proof generation, execution-attestation admission, and publication prerequisites.
- Preserve or simplify the current review journal and current-format validation.
- Enforce the review-only role boundary through existing supported mechanisms and honest instructions.
- Reconcile affected open issues, accepted design/plan pointers, ACs, verification commands, dependencies, and aggregate completion obligations.

Out of scope:

- A new generic evidence engine, cryptographic attestation service, schema framework, policy engine, database redesign, or migration program.
- Implementation or delivery by participants in an AIPR review session.
- Unrelated knowledge retrieval, analytics, experimentation, or global-install redesign.
- Activation of #107, Windows support, or operating on protected unsettled sessions.

## 3. Product boundary

### 3.1 Review and write behavior

- AIPR routes the selected artifact and relevant context to review participants and collects their responses, findings, dispositions, and recorded decisions.
- Review participants may write only their designated current review-documentation outputs. They may not edit application source, dependencies, configuration, Git metadata, another participant's response, or earlier sealed records.
- AIPR-owned writes are limited to its declared coordination state and designated review documentation. AIPR does not implement source changes or commit the reviewed codebase.
- If an external author revises the artifact, AIPR records the new artifact identity/version and a subsequent review. That revision belongs to the external authoring workflow, not an AIPR permission to modify the subject. The registered submitting participant supplies the revised artifact reference and expected prior review revision/subject digest through an explicit handoff. AIPR reads and snapshots those bytes; it does not apply a patch to the subject. Existing role/session authentication remains required.
- An artifact change during a reviewer turn must refuse stale advancement and cannot silently replace the reviewed subject. Preserve recorded responses and snapshots as history about their original digests; a later edit does not retroactively erase or relabel that history. While drift is unresolved, preserve the unfinished turn as incomplete. If the external author restores the exact authorized bytes, the existing valid submission/retry path remains available; AIPR does not restore the subject itself. Any supersession requires an adopted operation under the applicable versioned contract. A revision handoff must bind the new digest, preserve prior responses, reject stale submissions and retain duplicate-safe retries. An unchanged-artifact response remains possible with an explicit disposition; neither a revision nor a new acceptance is inferred from silence. The exact supported existing operation or necessary versioned change must be adopted before its implementation.
- Review-only participant behavior is a separate workstream from process observation and owner recovery. Current author artifact-write instructions, CLI submission behavior, launch restrictions, and #118/#148–#150 author-capable requirements must be reconciled together; removing a write permission alone is insufficient.
- Existing flows that let an AIPR author participant edit the subject must be assessed explicitly against this boundary. Removing proof alone does not resolve that conflict.
- Supported provider path restrictions may enforce the boundary. Existing manual sessions remain instruction-bound where the host offers no enforcement. Do not build a new sandbox or claim that instructions prove write denial.
- A controller cannot manufacture a participant's findings or acceptance. Exit, silence, timeout, successful JSON validation, and schema compatibility are not review acceptance.

### 3.2 What records establish

A record describes the activity reported and recorded by AIPR: the artifact version, participants, turns, findings, responses, decisions, timestamps when available, and recorded outcome. Validation establishes supported format and internal consistency. Artifact hashes associate records with bytes and can detect changed bytes when compared with retained values.

These checks do not independently establish provider execution, model execution, uncompromised host behavior, or a cryptographic reviewer identity. Requested, declared, observed, unavailable, and uncertain values retain their original meanings. Do not strengthen them by changing labels.

### 3.3 Coordination mechanisms to preserve

Keep mechanisms needed for correct moderation:

- Review/project/role/session/turn association and the existing local authentication needed to prevent misrouting.
- One writer for a state transition, expected-revision checks, atomic persistence, and duplicate-safe retry behavior.
- Artifact/response version association, prior-response preservation, and explicit lifecycle outcomes.
- Read-only diagnosis and supported recovery of a pending turn without launching duplicate participants.
- Ownership checks that prevent taking a live lock or terminating an unrelated process. Timeout or endpoint silence alone does not establish process death.
- Ordinary supported filesystem access and containment checks for AIPR's own writes.

Keep a mechanism because a named behavior requires it, not because an old proof contract requires a field. Development issue approvals and CI integrity remain separate from the product journal; this refactoring does not bypass the repository's governed delivery workflow.

## 4. Versioned record contract

### 4.1 Use existing formats first

Existing `schemas/event-v1.json`, `schemas/event-v2.json`, `schemas/manifest-v1.json`, participant/response schemas, renderers, and validators are the starting point. Do not introduce a new format merely to rename the system.

Inventory each currently emitted durable document. Its machine-readable data must identify its schema family and version, interpreted in its documented operation/document-family context. Human-readable review documents must carry a documented schema/format identifier in their metadata. The associated schema document must explain structure and field meanings. A package version or producer image digest is not a schema version.

Add missing metadata only in the appropriate versioned format. A breaking field or semantic change requires a new schema version; do not silently change the meaning of an already published version. Historical schema definitions and their explanatory documents remain available. The same versioning rule applies to process-source class/ledger, conformance/registration and persisted ownership/creation-source records, not only review documents. Removing required review-approval fields from a class requires a new class version; an affected ledger or persisted seal contract likewise requires an explicit versioned change. Preserve the original v1 schemas, ledger bytes and recorded seals as history rather than rewriting them under their old identifiers. Fixing documentation errors may be additive with an explicit erratum; do not rewrite historical bytes to make an old record validate differently.

### 4.2 Current reader and writer behavior

- The current writer emits the current documented version for each document family.
- The current validator dispatches through a fixed package-owned set of supported operation/document-family and schema-identifier combinations. The API envelope uses schema artifact `ai-peer-review.api-response/v1` while its transport tag is `ai-peer-review.response/v1`; the current participant-response writer also uses that transport identifier with `schemas/response-v1.json`. Participant response-v1 remains a supported existing/historical format with unchanged semantics. New reviews that implement §4.3 use an explicitly adopted participant response-v2 schema for its mandatory protected provenance metadata; v1 is not silently extended or reinterpreted. Preserve contextual dispatch, both existing schema artifacts and the distinct API envelope semantics. A tag alone must not select a reader.
- Use existing event validation, event reduction and document-specific checks for journal interpretation. `src/api/validate.mjs:validateContract` is an API-contract subset, not a JSON Schema evaluator for journal documents: it does not resolve `$ref` or evaluate `anyOf`, and its `oneOf` branch returns before sibling constraints. Do not route journal schemas through it or advertise full JSON Schema validation without a separately justified, bounded implementation. Existing API validation is not weakened by this cleanup.
- A fixed package-owned registry may select only supported local document checks. An input cannot select executable code, a retained runtime, or a remote schema download.
- Validate required fields, supported values, associated review/artifact/turn references, and applicable recorded transition consistency.
- Distinguish malformed input, unsupported format, incomplete records, and well-formed records with a non-accepted outcome using each existing format's real enums. A reviewer rejection or revision request is valid response/event data; it need not be a terminal journal status. Existing `buildManifest`/manifest-v1 accepts terminal acceptance variants only. Pending, abandoned, superseded or otherwise non-accepted event histories are inspected at their journal/response boundary, not forced into acceptance manifests. Introducing another terminal outcome or changing manifest semantics requires an explicit versioned decision. A recorded non-acceptance is not itself malformed.
- Validation must not require provider execution, model attestation, an exact installed producer image, a source-class approval, a Git finalization commit as evidence of reviewer acceptance, a primary enrollment operation, or a published proof bundle.
- Unknown/future schemas produce an explicit unsupported-format result without modifying the input. Missing schema metadata is reported as such; no guessed version or automatic migration.
- Existing historical readers may remain where useful and inexpensive. New development is not obligated to add historical runtime replay, retained-image execution, automatic migration, or universal old-version acceptance.
- Validation of a recorded outcome does not approve an artifact or satisfy a consuming system's approval policy. External consumers make their own explicit workflow decisions from the recorded data.

### 4.3 Repository context and acceptance provenance

Repository commit provenance is useful review context, separate from execution attestation or source-class approval. Record full commit object IDs, never abbreviated SHAs or moving branch names as substitutes. A commit identifies a repository snapshot; the FUR digest identifies the exact reviewed bytes, including frontmatter. Neither value substitutes for the other. A dirty worktree can contain several FUR revisions at the same commit.

- **Initial manifest and stable review root:** Create the versioned initial manifest when the review session is created, before opening its first reviewer turn. When that manifest is completed/sealed, capture the subject repository's then-current full HEAD as `review_root_commit`, together with repository/branch identity (explicitly distinguishing detached HEAD), creation time and the submitted FUR revision/digest. In the same capture window record a bounded working-state observation: the exact non-FUR/non-collateral staged, unstaged and nonignored untracked paths, their existence/type/mode and content digests for index and working-tree state as applicable, or an explicit clean marker; unsupported, unreadable or unstable state is explicitly unavailable with its reason. The root commit plus these overlays identify the observed working context without retaining the whole repository or source-file contents as review collateral. Tracked paths remain included even if an ignore rule matches them; ignored untracked paths, installed dependencies and other external runtime state are outside this comparison, and a clean result makes no claim about them. Verify HEAD, submitted bytes, path inventory and the recorded working-state digests remain stable across capture; retry or report unavailable context rather than writing an inconsistent root. The SHA is the HEAD observed while completing the manifest, not the future commit that will contain it; no self-referential commit is required. Preserve the immutable initial manifest as collateral, with later records linked to its identity/digest. This is a repository-context anchor, not a retained initial FUR snapshot or cumulative patch baseline. Preserve the earliest retained FUR's original writing-basis commit through its lineage metadata, separately from the session root; if the FUR was written before session creation, those SHAs may differ. Historical missing origin metadata remains unavailable.
- **Drift at acceptance and code-context forks:** Record the `review_root_commit` to acceptance-HEAD comparison separately from the effective working-context comparison: compare the root commit plus its recorded working overlay with the acceptance commit plus an equivalently captured overlay. Record pre-existing dirty paths separately from paths whose effective content, existence, type or mode changed since the root; committing unchanged dirty content changes commit/index bookkeeping, not the evaluated working content, whereas discarding that content is a context change. An unavailable overlay or required Git object makes the affected comparison unavailable, never clean by inference. The immutable initial manifest and each subsequent sealed linked record declare the exact contained FUR/collateral paths they introduce; derive the comparison exclusions from that complete verified chain, including both old and new FUR paths for a supported recorded rename. Resolve links and path roles before exclusion; a missing, corrupt or ambiguous link makes classification unavailable. Do not use directory exclusions, unexpanded templates or filename resemblance to conceal source, configuration, test or tracked dependency changes. Other sessions' documentation is outside this session's exclusion set and is reported as other changed paths in an informational divergence record; the reasoned suitability assessment may establish that verified documentation is irrelevant without silently expanding the set. Record compared SHAs, overlay identities, changed paths and classification. If the effective comparison is complete and the only net changes are the FUR and declared collateral, keep the same review root and record `review-only` drift (including an explicit no-change case); ordinary publication commits do not create a new code basis. If other effective file states differ, retain the original root and append a linked code-context divergence record containing the observed HEAD, affected paths and any observed merge/rebase context. This is an informational fork of the specification's code context, not a command to create a Git branch, reject an otherwise valid participant decision, or rewrite the old root. Record non-ancestor/history-rewritten cases even when endpoint code trees are equivalent; ancestry changes alone do not prove code changed. Distinguish observed endpoint content differences from any stronger claim about every intervening commit. Unresolvable roots or incomplete comparisons are `unavailable`, never assumed review-only. These records do not waive the within-turn stale HEAD/FUR checks below; drift between sealed turns and historical acceptance-to-current drift have different timing from a change during an open turn.
- **Review-response Markdown:** Every newly written response under the new participant response-v2 contract includes protected `reviewer_commit` metadata, bound to its review, turn and FUR digest. AIPR captures the full HEAD from the subject checkout bound to that reviewer session at submission; the reviewer establishes and acknowledges the context actually evaluated through the versioned submission handoff, not by editing generated protected frontmatter. AIPR compares that acknowledged context, current checkout HEAD and reviewed FUR digest during sealing and refuses stale submission if any changes; the reviewer must reevaluate and acknowledge the final context before retrying. Authorized external review publication must wait while a reviewer turn is open, as defined in §4.4; there is no publication-only exception to this HEAD check. Unrelated human work remains possible and is handled as drift, not prohibited by a global worktree freeze. In the default same-physical-worktree topology, the captured commit legitimately equals that worktree's shared HEAD. A controller's value from another checkout or another time is not a substitute. Supporting a separate reviewer checkout requires an adopted versioned checkout binding and repository-identity contract before use; until then it is explicitly unsupported. Existing `artifact_commit` retains its submitted-artifact meaning. The new field uses a discriminated available record with the full commit ID, or an explicit unavailable record with a reason; unavailable is never encoded as legacy `null`/no-commit. Preserve a recorded participant decision if capture is unavailable while reporting provenance as incomplete. The new reader/writer and handoff contracts must specify these states before implementation.
- **FUR Markdown:** Every externally authored revision of the File Under Review includes a unique, stable `revision_id` within its artifact lineage and `based_on_commit` in its YAML frontmatter: the subject repository HEAD used as the basis when that revision was written. Set it before snapshotting or reviewing the revision, and preserve it with the exact revision bytes. An unchanged FUR retains its original basis even if the branch later advances. The external author updates this metadata when writing a revision; AIPR does not edit the FUR to add it. At new-format intake and every revision handoff, validate the Markdown frontmatter and the retained lineage: refuse missing/malformed metadata, changed bytes with an unchanged revision ID, or reuse of an earlier ID for different bytes. Reuse is allowed only for byte-identical bytes; a retry must also match its original review/turn/version. A fresh ID must not alias an earlier revision. Refusal preserves the existing chain and directs the external author to correct the FUR; AIPR cannot repair it. The explicit historical-predecessor representation in §4.4 does not waive metadata for a newly authored revision. The writing-basis SHA does not mean the FUR already exists in that commit. The external publication workflow commits each completed review turn as specified in §4.4; AIPR itself does not commit the subject. For non-Markdown subjects, adopt an explicit equivalent versioned metadata representation before supporting this requirement; do not inject YAML into source files.
- **Acceptance manifest:** Each final acceptance of a FUR under SAR, SPR or XPR records `acceptance_head`, the subject branch HEAD at the time that acceptance is recorded. Bind every entry to its review ID, review pattern, accepted FUR digest/revision, recorded decision/basis and acceptance time. Retain separate entries for successive review patterns and review instances, even when their SHAs coincide. Preserve existing entries rather than overwriting them with the latest acceptance. Preserve the repository and branch context needed to interpret the SHA; distinguish a detached HEAD explicitly. A recorded review-pattern label does not strengthen participant independence or identity claims.
- Capture acceptance HEAD from the subject worktree at the acceptance transition, with a consistency check that refuses/retries if the relevant HEAD or accepted FUR changes during capture. Do not record a later documentation/publication commit as the earlier acceptance HEAD. A missing or unavailable acceptance SHA remains explicit and cannot satisfy the complete provenance requirement. Preserve any already recorded participant decision without presenting incomplete provenance as complete acceptance documentation. An acceptance SHA is an observation of repository context, not a Git finalization requirement or proof that the worktree was clean.
- **Before plan generation:** Select the applicable accepted review layers under the owner's approval policy and verify the current FUR digest against the accepted revision, using the retained diff chain where necessary to identify earlier accepted bytes. Evaluate repository drift from the specification's earliest available writing-basis commit and each applicable session's stable review root to current HEAD; acceptance HEADs and divergence records provide additional context, not substitute roots. For available session roots compare their recorded working overlays with an equivalently captured current working state. An earlier writing basis without a retained working overlay supports only a commit comparison; report that limit, never invent a clean historical working state. Preserve the earliest-basis comparison as lineage context and explicitly account for changes already examined in later accepted revisions; do not demand repeated re-review solely because those changes remain visible from the older basis. Judge suitability against the applicable accepting sessions' evaluated contexts and their remaining unreviewed differences while retaining all required comparisons. Missing context that prevents that assessment takes the current-HEAD review path below. Record compared SHAs, working-observation identities, affected code areas and the effect on specification requirements, interfaces, dependencies and assumptions. Bind the named human/agent decider's suitability decision and rationale to the exact accepted FUR digest/revision and those comparison inputs. Materiality is this recorded judgement, not an AIPR line-count algorithm. Review-only changes or demonstrably irrelevant code changes need not invalidate the specification. Wide or material drift that undermines its suitability as a planning foundation requires specification review against current HEAD, with any necessary revision and fresh recorded acceptance, before generating the plan. Do not reduce suitability to a raw changed-line count or treat an informational fork as automatic failure. If rebase/squash makes the original code context unresolvable, record that limitation and review the specification against current HEAD before planning; recovering FUR bytes alone does not establish code-context suitability. A new current-context review has its own initial manifest/root and links to earlier history without overwriting it. No hidden requirement to retain every turn commit follows.
- **After plan acceptance and backlog hydration:** Preserve the accepted plan's identity, source specification/review links and recorded repository context in the backlog handoff. Subsequent code-drift evaluation belongs to each story's just-in-time deep dive before implementation. That analysis compares the story's relevant current code with its accepted planning basis, checks plan assumptions, interfaces and dependencies, and records what changed, why it matters and any scoped adjustment. Material conflicts, changed intent or invalid acceptance criteria go through the applicable governed revision/replanning workflow before implementation; drift is not permission to change scope silently. Do not require a global specification/plan re-review for every later commit merely because the backlog exists. Reassess the affected broader foundation when the deep dive discovers that its assumptions no longer hold. This assigns responsibility to the downstream workflow; AIPR supplies review records and does not execute story implementation or hydrate the backlog itself. The downstream ownership and registration gate are specified in §6; this draft does not claim that integration is already delivered.

Use the existing response, manifest, lineage and snapshot facilities where their documented semantics fit. Adopt explicit versioned field contracts and reader/writer changes wherever these requirements add mandatory metadata or change manifest structure; do not insert required fields into published v1 contracts silently. Retained historical responses, FUR snapshots and acceptance manifests remain unchanged. Report missing historical provenance as missing, rather than backfilling current HEAD or pretending a prior review accepted these new requirements. This specification's current frontmatter records its actual writing basis and revision ID; earlier acceptance remains scoped to the previously accepted bytes.

### 4.4 Review collateral, turn commits and reconstruction

Required review collateral comprises (1) review-response Markdown, (2) author-response Markdown, (3) versioned manifest records and (4) one separately named reversible diff for each changed retained FUR revision. The revised FUR is committed with the cycle's collateral by the authorized external publisher. The manifest is part of the retained review package. Adopt a new manifest version for in-progress/publication state and lineage links; preserve manifest-v1's terminal-acceptance semantics and old bytes. No implementation may insert these requirements into published v1 contracts silently.

- **Revision-chain unit and boundaries:** Retain each distinct FUR state submitted to a review, produced by a completed review cycle, or explicitly published between reviews under the new workflow. This is not every editor save or repository commit unrelated to the FUR. The external publication workflow must record any intermediate FUR state it commits; uncommitted intermediate edits may be represented by the boundary diff to the next submitted state. Each changed retained state has a unique revision ID and one forward diff from the exact preceding retained state, including frontmatter. Manifest entries record predecessor/result revision identities and byte digests, patch format identifier/version, direction, and unique diff path/digest. The first revision-producing cycle compares against the draft actually reviewed, whether committed, dirty or newly created; it cannot substitute different committed bytes or an empty file.
- **Cross-review and historical links:** At intake, record either an explicit lineage origin or the predecessor review/publication identity, its retained manifest path and digest, and predecessor revision identity/digest. Linked manifest records are sealed immutable files so later current-state updates cannot invalidate the link. Differing submitted bytes require a boundary diff from that predecessor to the new submitted state before the chain can be called complete. Such a boundary is recorded as external revision activity, not as a fabricated review cycle or acceptance. The external publisher supplies an authorized predecessor reference and exact bytes; AIPR verifies them and emits the boundary record/diff as designated documentation. An externally published state not yet reviewed is labelled unreviewed and cannot inherit an earlier acceptance. A predecessor predating revision frontmatter uses a manifest-recorded legacy identifier plus exact digest, explicitly marked as lacking embedded metadata; never backfill its bytes. If prior bytes or links are unavailable, preserve that gap and stop the complete-reconstruction claim. These prospective requirements do not imply that historical intermediate drafts were retained or reviewed.
- **Diff ownership and exact inputs:** AIPR produces diffs as designated review documentation at declared contained paths; neither reviewer nor author participant gains arbitrary file-write permission. Its only inputs are the exact sealed predecessor/result bytes whose digests match the associated records, including the reviewed initial snapshot and author-handoff snapshot. For an external boundary, verify and seal the supplied predecessor and new bytes before generation. Never substitute Git HEAD, the index or a later working-tree read. Retain the coordination inputs until the verified diff and linked records are durable and publication is settled. Existing internal snapshots may serve this purpose without becoming a permanent per-revision archival requirement. An explicitly adopted patch format/version is recorded in the manifest so raw patch bytes need no extra metadata header.
- **Completed cycle:** A revision cycle consists of the reviewer's sealed response on revision N and the submitting participant's sealed disposition response that introduces revision N+1 or explicitly retains N. An acceptance cycle consists of the accepting reviewer response and an unchanged-FUR closing acknowledgment/disposition from the submitting participant; the latter records acceptance receipt, not a second reviewer decision. Missing responses leave cycle collateral explicitly incomplete; no controller fabricates participant prose. Preserve the different subject digest/revision for each response. An unchanged-artifact cycle retains the same ID/digest and records an explicit empty change result. A final participant decision can be recorded before its closing acknowledgment or external publication is complete; these are separate states.
- **Exact reversible patches:** The adopted format must preserve exact content, encoding and line endings. Preserve file addition, deletion or path changes explicitly where supported; unsupported subjects fail explicitly. Before recording a successful diff, verify both forward application from the sealed predecessor and reverse application from the sealed result in separate destinations, checking exact input/output digests. Reject fuzzy or lossy application, including newline/encoding conversion. Unique revision/turn filenames preserve every patch. The concrete patch representation and supported subject set must be selected and behavior-tested in the bounded implementation plan; a binary-safe Git patch is a candidate, not an unverified format guarantee.
- **External publication and mode compatibility:** New-format reviews remain journal-only from AIPR's perspective. Their publication is a separate authorized external workflow; AIPR and review participants never stage, commit or push. The new contract permits publication to remain pending across subsequent cycles, but the publisher may change the shared HEAD only in a coordinated window between sealed turns or after review completion, when no reviewer turn is open. While a reviewer turn is open, AIPR reports publication as waiting and the external publisher does not change HEAD. Before publishing, the external workflow verifies and reserves that boundary through the adopted versioned lifecycle; a new reviewer turn cannot open until the publication step completes or releases its boundary safely. An interrupted/uncertain publication preserves the reservation and pending state until explicit reconciliation; do not infer completion or launch a conflicting turn. This is coordination for the authorized publisher, not a freeze on unrelated human work. Any uncoordinated HEAD change during review still triggers the strict stale-context refusal in §4.3. For each completed cycle the publisher makes a dedicated commit containing both responses, that cycle's sealed manifest record and diff (or explicit empty result), and the exact cycle-result FUR. If the working FUR has advanced, obtain the cycle's exact result from retained sealed bytes or verified reconstruction and verify the staged FUR digest against the manifest before committing. Do not stage the later working FUR by accident, rewrite its bytes merely to publish history, or include unrelated staged changes. Publish pending cycles in chain order with duplicate-aware retries. Failed or interrupted publication stays pending; it neither erases a participant decision nor implies success. AIPR checks designated documentation and reports publication state; the external publisher verifies the exact staged/committed content under its own authorized Git workflow. Publication records may report the outcome without becoming a required turn-commit SHA index. Existing `normal` records retain their recorded Git-bound behavior under compatible legacy maintenance, not conversion; their automation-owned commits cannot be treated as the new role boundary. Existing `no-commit` records likewise retain their sealed contract, with any current external archival work described honestly. New-format contracts must explicitly allow external publication and resulting HEAD changes between/after sealed turns while preserving stale-turn checks; do not silently retrofit old startup/index invariants. Neither old mode by itself satisfies this new workflow.
- **Squash-safe retention and reconstruction:** Retain the latest chain-head FUR and all uniquely named diffs, responses and linked immutable manifest records as ordinary files in the merged parent/default tree. The chain head can be accepted, unaccepted or explicitly published unreviewed; its status is recorded separately. Starting from that exact head, verify its manifest digest, then reverse diffs in chain order through cross-review boundaries. Check every digest and every embedded revision ID where the version requires it; legacy predecessors use their recorded identifier/digest without rewriting them. Report which reconstructed revisions were actually accepted and under which review/pattern. If the head is the final accepted FUR, it and the chain suffice; if later unreviewed states have been published, their head and boundary diffs first recover that accepted FUR. Preserve the retained head while reconstructing elsewhere. No separate baseline, cumulative patch, permanent per-revision snapshot, original review-turn commit or per-revision Git object is required. Missing/corrupt links or diffs produce explicit incomplete/corrupt history, never guessed recovery.

FUR reconstruction and repository-context comparison are different capabilities. The retained package can reconstruct FUR revisions after squash, but FUR-only diffs cannot reconstruct the entire repository at an unreachable acceptance SHA. The pre-planning suitability check in §4.3 reports unavailable repository history and requires review against current HEAD when that context cannot be compared; it must not claim that reconstructing the FUR proves the surrounding code is unchanged.

### 4.5 Data minimization

Retain useful audit information without adding unrestricted provider output, credentials, private session handles, or private filesystem details to public exports. Existing sensitive historical records are not republished by this cleanup. A routine copy/export, if still required by a concrete consumer, is a versioned data interface rather than an attestation.
